A barcode prefix tells you where a product was made
who issued the number.

The leading digits of a GTIN identify the GS1 member organisation that allocated the number to the brand owner. A company registers with one organisation, usually where it has its seat, and keeps those number ranges wherever it later manufactures.

The source

The opposite belief is widespread. It travels in chain letters urging people to boycott a particular prefix, and most lookup services reinforce it by labelling the field country_of_origin. This dataset calls it issuing_organisation, and organisation_country is where that organisation sits, not where the goods came from. If you build on this data, please keep those names.

Method

Reconciled against two sources rather than copied from one: GS1’s own prefix list, and English Wikipedia’s List of GS1 country codes. Where they disagree, GS1’s reading is used and the disagreement is kept in the record instead of being quietly resolved. Two ranges are marked contested, and carry source_disagreement as true so you can filter for them without reading prose.

If you maintain your own copy, check the date on your source. Several GS1 member organisations publish stale lists; GS1 Slovakia’s still names Serbia and Montenegro. A stale list looks exactly like a current one.
